# Page decision rules

Every meaningful keyword cluster ends in one of five decisions.

## KEEP

Use when the current page already owns the intent cleanly and performance is healthy enough that rewriting risks more than it gains.

## IMPROVE

Default action when a relevant page already exists. Typical triggers:

- meaningful impressions but weak position;
- page-two/page-three rankings;
- near-page-one rankings with weak CTR;
- another page is ranking for an intent that belongs here;
- title/H1/copy/internal links do not make the page's purpose clear;
- useful local proof, pricing clarity, FAQs or conversion information is missing.

## MERGE

Use when two or more pages substantially answer the same intent and there is no durable reason for both to exist independently. Preserve the strongest URL and redirect or canonicalize deliberately after checking backlinks, traffic and indexation.

## CREATE

Create a new page only when all are true:

1. There is evidence of search demand or a strategically important customer need.
2. No existing page can satisfy the intent cleanly with an improvement.
3. The new page has a distinct job in the information architecture.
4. The business can provide genuinely useful, specific content for that intent.
5. The page will not simply be a near-copy with a location or service token changed.

## IGNORE

Use when demand is negligible, intent is irrelevant, the query is misleading, or winning the query would not reasonably support the business.

## Priority scoring

Scripts may calculate an opportunity score, but the score is a sorting aid rather than an automatic publishing order.

Prefer:

- commercial intent;
- high impressions;
- positions roughly 4–30;
- low CTR relative to the position;
- a clear existing owner page;
- services/markets the business actually wants to sell.

Avoid creating content just because a numeric score is high.
